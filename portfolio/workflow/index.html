Workflow

Engineering judgment first. AI second.

I use AI because it is useful. I do not use it as a substitute for architecture, product reasoning, review, or production responsibility.

1. Assess

Inspect before changing

I start by reading the existing system, constraints, and failure modes. Fast guesses are cheap. Cleaning up the damage from bad guesses is not.

2. Shape

Reduce ambiguity early

I define the problem, tighten the scope, and call out tradeoffs before implementation drifts into vague motion.

3. Build

Prefer structure that survives

I favor maintainable boundaries, clear ownership, and delivery patterns that can keep moving without turning the codebase into a negotiation.

4. Review

Correctness is not delegated

Changes still need technical scrutiny, product judgment, and operational thinking. Shipping faster is only useful if the result still deserves to exist.

Where AI helps

Acceleration, not abdication

  • Planning and option shaping when a problem space is still broad
  • Drafting implementation paths and reducing boilerplate startup cost
  • Refactoring support and codebase exploration
  • Synthesis across docs, code, and open decisions
  • Review support when checking consistency and obvious gaps
What stays with me

Engineer-owned responsibilities

  • Architecture and system boundaries
  • Product tradeoffs and implementation judgment
  • Correctness and review quality
  • Production accountability
  • Deciding what not to build
Modernization stance

Most systems do not need reinvention. They need honesty.

A lot of codebases are not failing because the stack is old. They are failing because boundaries are weak, ownership is blurry, and every new feature has to tunnel through accumulated compromises. Good modernization work fixes the actual drag instead of repainting the front end and calling it strategy.

Planning, implementation, review AI is useful. Judgment still matters more.
