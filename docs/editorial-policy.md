# Public editorial policy

The site exists to provide credible engineering evidence. It is not a place to inflate private work into public claims.

## Evidence standard

Every claim must be one of:

1. Independently verifiable through a public artifact such as this site or Ordr.now.
2. Personally attestable, stated conservatively without invented outcomes or metrics.
3. A generalized engineering method with identifying implementation details removed.
4. An opinion, proposal, or future plan labeled clearly as such.

“Planned,” “proposed,” and “not yet verified” are required when a capability or result does not exist as public evidence. Passing source checks must not be described as runtime, device, deployment, or production proof.

## Confidentiality boundary

Do not publish current client identities, product names, repositories, internal domains, preview environments, tickets, tenants, customers, screenshots, private scripts, prompts, operating procedures, authentication internals, proprietary architecture, or non-public metrics. Do not reconstruct a client implementation by combining otherwise generalized details.

General methods may describe intent control, repository context, bounded execution, verification layers, recovery limits, and human approval boundaries. Examples that require concrete implementation details should use independently owned Ordr.now work or a clearly labeled synthetic example.

Personally attestable experience may describe responsibilities and methods the author performed. Independently verifiable claims should link to the public evidence. Neither category permits confidential details or unsupported impact claims.

## Editorial test

Before publication, remove hype, repeated conclusions, generic AI language, vague abstractions, unsupported staff-level claims, and sentences that imply agents remove engineering judgment. State limitations and proof boundaries plainly. If a claim cannot survive a direct evidence question, qualify it, label it as planned, or remove it.
